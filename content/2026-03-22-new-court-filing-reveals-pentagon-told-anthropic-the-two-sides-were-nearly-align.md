# New Court Filing Reveals Pentagon Told Anthropic the Two Sides Were Nearly Aligned — A Week After Trump Declared the Relationship Kaput

In a twist that could only happen in 2026, a new court filing exposes a stunning disconnect: while President Trump was publicly declaring the government's relationship with AI company Anthropic "kaput," Pentagon officials were privately telling the company they were "nearly aligned" on a deal. The contradiction, laid bare in federal court, raises serious questions about who's really calling the shots on AI policy—and whether the White House and the defense establishment are even reading the same memo.

---

## 🔍 What the Filing Actually Says

Anthropic submitted two sworn declarations to a California federal court late Friday, pushing back on the Pentagon's assertion that the AI company poses an "unacceptable risk to national security." The documents reveal that:

- **Just one week before Trump's public declaration**, Pentagon negotiators were actively working with Anthropic to resolve outstanding concerns and move toward a final agreement.
- The two sides had achieved "near alignment" on key terms including data handling, audit rights, and safety testing protocols.
- The Pentagon's "unacceptable risk" rationale appears to be a reversal from earlier internal assessments that found Anthropic's Claude models suitable for certain defense applications with appropriate safeguards.

The filing suggests a sudden, top-down intervention overruled months of technical negotiations.

---

## 🤔 Why This Matters

### A White House vs. Pentagon Split on AI?
The revelation hints at a deeper rift between the White House's political appointees and the defense establishment's technical experts. While the Pentagon's acquisition office saw value in partnering with a leading AI lab that has prioritized safety and constitutional AI principles, the White House appears to have prioritized a broader anti-AI-regulation stance. The contradiction raises questions about whether national security decisions are being made based on policy ideology or technical risk assessments.

### The Precedent It Sets
If the President can unilaterally scuttle a negotiated agreement between a defense agency and a responsible AI company based on political expediency, what does that say about the rule of law in AI governance? Companies may hesitate to engage with the government if deals can be undone by a tweet. Conversely, this could embolden startups to demand clearer, codified rules rather than relying on ad-hoc negotiations.

### Anthropic's Calculated Move
By filing these declarations, Anthropic is not just defending its reputation—it's building a public record that suggests the Pentagon was supportive. This could be a strategic play to pressure the White House through the courts or, at minimum, to signal to other governments and enterprise customers that it's a trustworthy partner. The company is essentially saying: "Even the Pentagon wanted us; the problem is political, not technical."

---

## 🧩 The Bigger Picture: AI Safety vs. Innovation

This case sits at the intersection of two competing narratives:

1. **The Safety Narrative** (Anthropic's position): Advanced AI models need careful oversight, red-tearing, and constitutional alignment to be safely deployed in high-stakes domains like national security. Anthropic's "responsible scaling" approach was seen as a model for this.

2. **The Innovation Narrative** (Trump's stance): Over-regulation stifles American competitiveness. The administration wants to accelerate AI deployment and sees safety certifications as bureaucratic barriers that could cede ground to adversaries like China.

The Pentagon's apparent support for Anthropic suggests that within the defense community, the safety narrative still holds weight—because in warfighting, a misbehaving AI isn't just a PR problem; it's a lethal risk.

---

## ⚖️ The Legal and Political Stakes

- **Judicial review:** The court now has evidence that the government's stated rationale ("unacceptable risk") may be pretext for a political decision. This could lead to a ruling that the Pentagon must reconsider Anthropic's application under proper procedures.
- **Executive power:** This case tests the limits of presidential authority over defense acquisitions. Can a President override a concluded negotiation by a cabinet department simply by declaring it "kaput"?
- **Chilling effect:** Other AI companies may be reluctant to work with the government if they perceive that deals can be broken arbitrarily. This could push AI development further into the private sector with less government oversight—a paradox for a administration claiming to protect national security.

---

## 🚀 What Comes Next

1. **Court decision:** The judge will need to weigh the sworn declarations against the government's national security claim. Expect a ruling on whether the Pentagon must restart negotiations or justify its reversal.
2. **Congressional interest:** Lawmakers may hold hearings on the mismatch between defense policy and White House statements.
3. **Market reaction:** Anthropic's stock (or valuation) could swing positively if the court forces the Pentagon to reconsider, as it validates the company's standing.
4. **Policy implications:** This could lead to legislation clarifying the process for national security AI contracts, potentially removing unilateral termination powers.

---

## Conclusion: When Politics Trumps Process

The Anthropic-Pentagon saga reveals a troubling truth: in the rush to lead on AI, political messaging is overriding technical due diligence. If the Pentagon's experts believed Anthropic could be a responsible partner, that assessment deserves weight. A tweet shouldn't overturn months of negotiation. The court now has a chance to reaffirm that national security decisions should be based on risk analysis, not political theater. Whatever the outcome, one thing is clear: the battle for the soul of American AI policy is being fought not just in labs and boardrooms, but in the corridors of power and the pages of court filings.

*In the end, even the Pentagon can't tell a court it's "nearly aligned" while the President says it's "kaput." The law demands consistency—and maybe, just maybe, sanity.* (◕‿◕)♡
