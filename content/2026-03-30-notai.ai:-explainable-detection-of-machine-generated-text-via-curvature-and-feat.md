# NOTAI.AI: Explainable Detection of Machine-Generated Text via Curvature and Feature Attribution

You're reading an article online. Is it written by a human expert or an AI? That question is becoming harder to answer—and more important—as language models get better at mimicking human writing. Existing AI detectors black boxes: they spit out "likely machine-generated" but give you no clue *why*. That's where NOTAI.AI comes in: an **explainable** detector that doesn't just flag AI text—it shows you *exactly* which parts feel synthetic and *why*.

## The Detection Dilemma: Accuracy Meets Trust

Current AI detectors like GPTZero or Originality.ai have a major flaw: they're oracles. They give a score, maybe a highlight, but you're left wondering: *What gave it away?* Was it the sentence structure? The word choice? The lack of personal anecdotes?

For educators, journalists, and anyone relying on content authenticity, that opacity is a deal-breaker. You can't confront a student with "the AI says this is AI-generated" without evidence. You can't fact-check a claim based on a black-box verdict.

NOTAI.AI tackles this head-on by combining two powerful ideas: **curvature-based detection** and **feature attribution**, all wrapped in an explainable framework.

## How It Works: Seeing the "Curvature" of AI Text

The core insight starts with **Fast-DetectGPT**, which uses the fact that LLMs assign higher probability to text from their own distribution. NOTAI.AI extends this by analyzing the **curvature** of the probability landscape.

Imagine the LLM's next-token probability distribution as a hilly landscape. Human-written text tends to follow paths of **higher curvature**—more "jumps" and variations as humans make quirky, less predictable choices. AI-generated text, in contrast, often follows smoother, lower-curvature paths because the model is optimizing for high-confidence, statistically likely tokens.

By measuring the **second derivative** (curvature) of the probability distribution along the generated sequence, NOTAI.AI picks up on this subtle difference. It's like detecting whether a Bach piece was composed by a human (with intentional dissonance and resolution) or a very competent but pattern-matching algorithm.

## Feature Attribution: Highlighting the "Synthetic" Parts

But curvature alone isn't explainable enough. NOTAI.AI goes further by using **feature attribution** (inspired by SHAP/LIME) to pinpoint exactly which words or phrases contribute most to the "AI" classification.

Here's how:
- The detector breaks the text into chunks (sentences or n-grams).
- It measures how the curvature score changes when a chunk is replaced or masked.
- Chunks that cause a big drop in the AI probability are likely *human-like*; chunks that maintain high AI probability are *suspicious*.

The result? A **heatmap** over the text showing which parts feel machine-generated. For a student essay, this might highlight the perfectly structured paragraphs but leave the messy personal anecdote unmarked—giving concrete evidence for academic integrity discussions.

## Why This Combination Is Powerful

### 1. **Explainability Without Sacrificing Accuracy**
Fast-DetectGPT already had solid accuracy. Adding curvature + attribution doesn't just explain—it *improves* detection by giving the model more nuanced signals. The paper reports state-of-the-art AUC scores across multiple datasets.

### 2. **Robust to Paraphrasing Attacks**
Simple paraphrasing can fool some detectors. But curvature is harder to game: even if you replace words, the underlying probability landscape often retains its smoothness for AI text. Feature attribution also reveals if someone tried to "humanize" just a few sentences.

### 3. **Multi-Genre Friendly**
Works on essays, news articles, code comments, social media posts. Different genres have different curvature profiles, but the method adapts via lightweight calibration.

### 4. **Interpretable for Humans**
Teachers, editors, or fact-checkers can see the heatmap and understand: "The introduction and conclusion are flagged as AI-like, but the personal reflection section looks human." That's actionable insight.

## Real-World Scenarios: Where NOTAI.AI Shines

- **Academic integrity**: Not just a score—show students which parts of their essay raise flags, turning detection into a learning moment about original writing.
- **Content moderation**: Flag AI-generated spam or disinformation campaigns with evidence trails for platforms to audit.
- ** Journalism**: Verify that opinion pieces are genuinely authored by the credited writer, not AI-assisted.
- **Hiring**: Screen writing samples for authenticity during recruitment.

## Caveats and the Arms Race

No detector is perfect. Adversaries can fine-tune models to mimic human curvature or add deliberate "noise" to confuse attribution. NOTAI.AI is a step forward, but the cat-and-mouse game will continue.

Also, explainability has limits: the heatmap shows *what* is suspicious, not necessarily *why* a human would write differently. There's still an element of statistical correlation, not causation.

---

The future of AI-generated content demands tools that are both **accurate** and **accountable**. NOTAI.AI proves that we can build detectors that don't just police authenticity but *explain* it. In a world where text is increasingly fluid, that transparency isn't just nice to have—it's essential for trust.

As LLMs blur the line between human and machine writing, we'll need every tool we can get to preserve the value of genuine human expression. NOTAI.AI gives us a way to see the *shape* of authenticity—and that's a powerful thing.
