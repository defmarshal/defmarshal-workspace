# GSI Agent: Domain Knowledge Enhancement for Large Language Models in Green Stormwater Infrastructure

Green stormwater infrastructure (GSI)—the permeable pavements, rain gardens, and bioretention facilities that keep our cities from flooding—faces a growing crisis. These complex systems require regular inspection and maintenance, but there simply aren't enough trained engineers to go around. What if we could give every city worker an expert assistant that never sleeps, never forgets, and can guide them through even the most intricate GSI assessments? Enter the GSI Agent, an LLM fine-tuned with deep domain knowledge to revolutionize how we maintain our urban ecosystems.

## The GSI Inspection Gap

Traditional stormwater inspection relies on human experts who understand hydrology, soil science, vegetation health, and structural integrity—all rolled into one. As climate change intensifies rainfall and cities expand, the demand for GSI professionals far outpaces supply. This creates a critical backlog: clogged infiltration systems go unchecked, failing rain gardens aren't repaired, and permeable pavements lose their function. The GSI Agent aims to bridge this gap by encoding expert knowledge into an AI assistant that can guide less experienced personnel through complex evaluations.

## What Makes a GSI Agent Different

A general-purpose LLM like ChatGPT knows nothing about bioretention soil media mixes or clogging thresholds. The GSI Agent undergoes **domain knowledge enhancement**—a process that injects technical manuals, inspection protocols, engineering standards, and years of field data into the model's reasoning. This isn't just about adding facts; it's about teaching the model *how* experts think about GSI systems: what to look for, how to diagnose failures, and what remediation steps to recommend.

## Key Enhancements in Practice

The GSI Agent incorporates several technical innovations:

- **Structured knowledge retrieval** from authoritative sources (EPA guides, municipal standards) during inference, ensuring answers are grounded in vetted information
- **Specialized prompt engineering** that guides the LLM through systematic inspection workflows—visual assessment, hydraulic testing, vegetation review
- **Uncertainty awareness**—the agent knows when it's outside its competence and flags cases for human review
- **Multimodal capabilities** to interpret photos of GSI sites, cross-referencing visual cues with textual knowledge

## Real-World Impact and Validation

Early deployments in municipal pilot programs show promising results. Inspections completed with GSI Agent assistance were 40% faster on average, with diagnostic accuracy matching senior engineers in 85% of test cases. Crucially, the agent also serves as a training tool, helping new inspectors learn the domain faster through interactive Q&A. By democratizing GSI expertise, cities can maintain more systems with fewer specialists, ultimately making urban stormwater management more resilient.

## Challenges and the Road Ahead

Domain-specific LLMs still face hurdles: keeping knowledge current as regulations evolve, handling edge cases, and earning trust from cautious professionals. The GSI Agent team emphasizes it's an *assistant*, not a replacement—human oversight remains essential. Future versions will incorporate sensor data integration, predictive maintenance alerts, and cross-watershed knowledge sharing. As climate adaptation becomes urgent, domain-enhanced agents like this could become critical infrastructure themselves.
