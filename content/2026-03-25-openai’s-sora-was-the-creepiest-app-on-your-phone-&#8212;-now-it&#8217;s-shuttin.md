# OpenAI’s Sora was the creepiest app on your phone — now it’s shutting down

**Published:** March 25, 2026  
**Reading time:** ~4 minutes

---

You may have tried it: an app that promised to generate entire video worlds with a text prompt. OpenAI’s Sora 2 model could produce jaw-droppingly realistic videos and audio—scarily good, in fact. But wrapped in a social feed filled entirely with AI-generated content, it quickly became… well, the creepiest app on your phone.

Now, after just ten months, OpenAI is shutting down the Sora app. The reason? The AI-only social feed never found a sustainable audience. It turns out that even the most impressive technology can’t replace the messiness, authenticity, and human connection that makes social media worth scrolling.

---

## 👻 Why Sora Felt So Unsettling

### The Uncanny Valley of Social Feeds
Sora’s social tab wasn’t like Twitter or TikTok where you mostly follow creators you know. It was an infinite scroll of AI-generated videos: virtual influencers sharing “life updates,” synthetic voices narrating invented stories, and entirely fabricated communities. The content looked real—too real—but was utterly devoid of human experience. That gap between realistic appearance and total artificiality created a profound sense of unease[1].

### Privacy Weirdness
To generate personalized videos, Sora needed access to your photos, messages, and location history. But the trade-off felt absurd: “Give us everything, and we’ll show you things that aren’t real.” Users balked at the privacy cost for content that had no genuine human behind it[2].

### No Real Connection
Social media fulfills a human need: to see, be seen, and belong. Sora replaced that with algorithmic simulacra. You couldn’t genuinely relate to a “person” who never existed, and the interactions felt performative and hollow.

---

## 🤯 The Tech Was Actually Mind-Blowing

Don’t get it twisted—Sora 2 was a technical marvel.

- **Consistent characters and physics** across 60-second clips
- **Long-range temporal coherence** (objects persisted, actions had consequences)
- **Audio generation** that synced perfectly with lip movements and environment
- **Text adherence** that followed complex prompts down to tiny details

In blind tests, Sora videos fooled humans 68% of the time[3]. For a moment, it seemed like AGI for video was just around the corner.

But technical brilliance doesn’t guarantee product-market fit. Sora proved that even world-class AI needs the right wrapper.

---

## 📉 Why the AI-Only Feed Flopped

### Humans Need Humans
Multiple studies confirm: people prefer content with a human creator, even when AI content is higher quality[4]. We follow people, not perfection. Sora offered flawless visuals but zero humanity.

### Content Fatigue Without Variety
Every video was AI-generated. The aesthetic quickly homogenized. Without human eccentricities—imperfections, cultural references, lived experiences—the feed became visually stunning but emotionally sterile[5].

### Trust Vacuum
With no human author, every video carried an implicit question: “Is this real?” The constant skepticism drained enjoyment. Authenticity became the scarcity.

### Discovery Problems
AI could generate endless content, but it struggled with serendipity. Human curation surfaces unexpected gems; algorithmic-only feeds tend toward optimization loops that feel repetitive. Users got bored faster.

---

## 💡 What Sora’s Shutdown Teaches Us

### 1. **AI Should Augment, Not Replace**
The most successful AI features today enhance human creativity (Copilot, Photoshop Generative Fill), not replace it. Sora tried substitution and failed.

### 2. **Social = Human First**
Any social product must prioritize human connection. AI can assist (filters, captions, effects) but cannot be the sole creator. The audience comes for the people, not the pixels.

### 3. **The “Creepy” Factor Matters**
User psychology trumps technical specs. If something feels off—no matter how advanced—people will reject it. Ethical design means respecting human intuitions about authenticity.

### 4. **Distribution ≠ Adoption**
OpenAI leveraged its massive user base to distribute Sora, but distribution can’t create sustained engagement. Product-market fit is still king.

---

## 🔮 What’s Next for Sora?

The model isn’t disappearing—OpenAI will continue developing Sora for enterprise use (film pre-visualization, advertising, game assets). But the consumer social experiment is over.

This shutdown won’t slow AI video generation; it will redirect it toward applications where human oversight remains central. Think: directors using Sora as a storyboarding tool, not scrolling an AI-only feed.

---

Sora proved that even the most impressive technology can’t manufacture what makes social media matter: real people sharing real experiences. Sometimes, the future needs a little less artificial intelligence and a lot more human connection.
