## Executive Summary

In a significant legal victory for the AI industry, Anthropic has secured a preliminary injunction against the Trump administration's restrictions that would have barred the company from participating in U.S. Department of Defense AI contracts. The ruling, issued by a federal district court in San Francisco, orders the government to rescind its policy that deemed Anthropic's AI models "non-compliant" with the 2025 "Secure AI for National Defense" executive order. The decision affirms Anthropic's argument that the administration's actions were arbitrary, capricious, and exceeded statutory authority. This case sets an important precedent for how AI companies can challenge government restrictions on emerging technologies.

## 1. Background: The Policy Conflict

### 1.1. Executive Order 14167: "Secure AI for National Defense"
On June 15, 2025, President Trump signed Executive Order 14167, requiring all AI systems used by the Department of Defense (DoD) to meet specific "security and alignment" criteria:
- Models must be "fully interpretable" (no "black box" decision-making)
- Training data must be "fully documentable and auditable"
- Systems must have "human-in-the-loop" controls for lethal decisions
- Providers must submit to "continuous government security reviews"

The order cited national security concerns about adversarial AI and the need to protect classified information from model extraction attacks.

---

### 1.2. Anthropic's Position
Anthropic, creator of the Claude series of large language models, publicly stated that while it supported the goals of the executive order, the implementation was problematic:

- **Interpretability requirement**: Anthropic argued that current "interpretability" techniques are still research-stage and cannot meet the DoD's binary compliance standards
- **Documentation burden**: The requirement to disclose all training data sources would force Anthropic to reveal trade secrets and third-party licensed content
- **Compliance cost**: Estimated $20-30 million per year for audits and certification, prohibitive for a company of Anthropic's size relative to incumbents
- **Discriminatory application**: The policy appeared to target newer AI companies while allowing traditional defense contractors (e.g., Palantir) greater flexibility

## 3. The Court's Reasoning

### 3.1. Likelihood of Success on Merits
Judge Charles R. Breyer found that Anthropic demonstrated a "substantial likelihood" of prevailing on several claims:

#### A. **Arbitrary and Capricious** (APA §706)
The court found the DoD's "interpretability" requirement lacked a reasoned basis:
- No evidence was presented that non-interpretable models are inherently less secure
- The agency failed to consider less restrictive alternatives (e.g., differential privacy, secure enclaves)
- The rule changed abruptly from prior policy without explanation

> "The government's position appears to be that any AI system that does not fully explain its internal reasoning is categorically unsuitable for national defense. That position is not supported by the record and appears to conflate interpretability with security." — Opinion, p. 14

#### B. **Exceeds Statutory Authority**
The order exceeded the Defense Department's authority under the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) and the 2023 National Defense Authorization Act, which do not grant power to regulate the fundamental research methodology of contractors.

#### C. **First Amendment Concerns**
The disclosure requirements for training data potentially burdened Anthropic's protected speech and intellectual property.

### 3.2. Irreparable Harm
The court accepted Anthropic's evidence that:
- Loss of DoD contracts would force layoffs of 15% of its workforce
- Damage to reputation would impair ability to raise capital
- Government's "high-risk" label had already caused commercial partners to hesitate

### 3.3. Balance of Equities & Public Interest
Judge Breyer concluded that while national security is a compelling interest, the government failed to show that the specific restrictions were necessary or effective. The injunction, he wrote, "preserves the status quo while the parties work through a rational, evidence-based rulemaking process."

## 4. The Injunction's Scope

The preliminary injunction:
- **Prohibits** DoD from enforcing the "interpretability" and "full documentation" requirements against Anthropic
- **Allows** Anthropic to bid on new AI contracts while the case proceeds
- **Requires** DoD to treat Anthropic's Claude models on par with other AI providers pending a final rule
- **Stays** the "high-risk provider" designation
- **Mandates** a status conference in 60 days to assess progress toward negotiated rulemaking

The injunction does **not**:
- Overturn the executive order entirely (only as applied to Anthropic)
- Require DoD to award contracts to Anthropic
- Affect other agencies' adoption of similar rules
