## What the Guidelines Require (Recap)

### 1. Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)
- All key creative decisions must involve human artists:
  - Character design final approval
  - Key animation frames ( sakuga )
  - Storyboard direction
  - Color styling
- AI-generated content must be clearly marked "AI-assisted" in credits
- Studios must maintain human artist headcount ≥ 60% of creative workforce

**Implementation checklist:**
- [ ] Document decision points where AI tools used
- [ ] Assign human approver for each AI-generated element
- [ ] Maintain sign-off records (who approved what, when)
- [ ] Calculate human/ AI ratio quarterly

### 2. AI Use Disclosure
Every episode must list:
```
AI Used for: [in-between/background/color/script]
AI Tool: [software name]
Human Oversight: [name/role]
```
**Penalty:** ¥500K - ¥5M per episode for failure to disclose

**Implementation checklist:**
- [ ] Update end-credit templates (all series currently in production)
- [ ] Create standard disclosure language for each AI tool
- [ ] Train production staff to track AI usage
- [ ] Build database to generate credit lines automatically

### 3. Retraining Budget
- Allocate **3% of production budget** to:
  - Up-skilling programs for animators
  - AI tool training
  - Mental health support
- Quarterly spending reports to JACA

**Implementation checklist:**
- [ ] Calculate 3% of current production budget (example: ¥1B budget → ¥30M retraining)
- [ ] Set up separate tracking account (audit trail)
- [ ] Plan programs: in-house training, external workshops, counseling services
- [ ] Document spending quarterly (due April 30, July 31, October 31, January 31)

### 4. Working Conditions
- Max 40 hours/week overtime (down from 80-100)
- Mandatory 1-day rest per week
- Income transparency: all employees on salary (no per-frame piece rate)

**Implementation checklist:**
- [ ] Audit current overtime hours; reduce to ≤40h
- [ ] Convert piece-rate workers to salaried (if applicable)
- [ ] Implement time tracking system
- [ ] Ensure at least one full day off per week per employee

## Final Compliance Sprint: 6-Day Plan

### Day 1-2 (March 28-29): Assessment & Documentation
1. **Download JACA self-assessment form** from https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/anime/jaca_self_assessment_2026.pdf (Japanese)
2. **Assemble compliance team** (producer, HR manager, production manager, legal counsel if available)
3. **Gather data:**
   - List all AI tools currently used (software names, versions, purposes)
   - Calculate 3% retraining budget (from current production budget)
   - Count creative workforce: human vs. AI-assisted (full-time equivalents)
   - Document overtime hours for past 6 months
   - Prepare credit templates with AI disclosure lines
4. **Complete self-assessment** — even if incomplete, submit what you have by March 31

### Day 3-4 (March 30-31): Submission & Buffer
1. **Submit self-assessment** via JACA online portal or email (before midnight JST March 31)
2. **Keep copy** of submission receipt
3. **Prepare brief explanation** of any items you couldn't comply with yet + timeline to fix
4. **If fully compliant:** Celebrate briefly, then prepare for audit
5. **If not compliant:** Consider requesting extension (unlikely but possible with compelling reason)
6. **Backup plan:** Apply for emergency METI bridge loan (¥50B fund) — requires separate application

### Day 5-6 (April 1-2): Post-Deadline Reality
1. **Monitor JACA website** for public compliance list publication (expected April 10)
2. **Check subsidy status** — if not submitted by March 31, expect Q2 funding freeze
3. **Communicate with stakeholders:**
   - TV stations: Inform them of your compliance status (may affect contracts)
   - International licensors: Reassure or renegotiate
   - Employees: Explain impact on working conditions and job security
4. **Begin implementing missing items** even if non-compliant — shows good faith

## What Happens If You Don't Comply?

### Immediate Consequences
- **Government subsidies frozen:** ¥2.3B annual fund withheld for Q2 and beyond
- **Public shaming:** JACA publishes non-compliant studio list (April 10)
- **TV contracts canceled:** Stations may cancel Q2-Q3 productions from non-compliant studios
- **Talent exodus:** Animators leave for compliant studios or other countries (Korea, China)

### Medium-term (June-September 2026)
- **30-50 studio closures** projected from subsidy loss + contract cancellation
- **Supply chain collapse:** Music labels, merch companies, voice actors lose work
- **Content shortage:** 20-30% fewer anime series in Fall 2026 season
- **Industry consolidation:** Survivors acquire distressed assets; mega-studios emerge

### Long-term (2027+)
- **Industry shrinks to 60%** of 2024 size
- **Quality paradox:** Fewer series, but higher average quality (resources concentrated)
- **Global outsourcing:** Japanese studios become "creative directors" only; production moves to lower-cost countries
- **Loss of cultural distinctiveness:** Anime becomes homogenized global product

## Survival Strategies for Non-Compliant Studios

### Option 1: Bridge Funding
- **METI emergency loan program:** ¥50B (~$325M) fund for compliance costs [1]
  - Application deadline: April 15, 2026
  - Max ¥50M per studio ($325K)
  - Low interest (1%); 5-year repayment
  - Requires compliance plan + audit
- **Alternative:** Private lenders, industry associations (AJA emergency fund)

### Option 2: Strategic Mergers
- Merge with compliant studio to share resources
- Acquire smaller non-compliant studios (bargain prices expected after April 1)
- Form cooperatives to pool compliance costs

### Option 3: Pivot Business Model
- Shift from TV series to **films** (fewer episodes, higher margins)
- Focus on **international co-productions** (Netflix/Disney direct commissions, higher budgets)
- **Specialize in high-end** (top 10% quality) rather than volume
- **Games & VR:** Diversify into interactive media (less regulated)

### Option 4: Relocate
- Move production to **Korea, China, Vietnam** where labor costs lower and regulations less strict
- Maintain creative direction in Japan, but outsource actual animation
- **Risk:** May lose "Japanese anime" cultural designation

## For Workers: What to Do

### Animators & Production Staff
1. **Check if your studio has submitted** self-assessment (ask producer)
2. **If no submission:** Consider job change to compliant studio before April 15 (mass layoffs likely after)
3. **Document overtime** — keep records of hours worked (use personal time tracker)
4. **Join union:** Japanese Animators Union (JAU) offering legal support: https://jau.or.jp
5. **Know your rights:** Labor laws still apply regardless of JACA; wage theft illegal
6. **Plan financially:** If studio likely to close, prepare 6-month emergency fund

### Voice Actors (Seiyuu)
- **Less directly affected** by JACA (not creative AI use yet)
- **But:** Studio closures = fewer roles
- **Diversify:** Radio, games, narration,海外 (overseas) work
- **Join union:** Japan Voice Actor Association (養成機関) for protections

## Conclusion

The anime industry stands at a precipice. The JACA guidelines, intended to protect workers and ensure ethical AI use, arrive at the worst possible moment — just as the industry suffers its worst financial crisis in history. The next 6 days will determine whether the industry can rally or faces a collapse that will reshape global animation for a decade.
